01 · Challenge

Citizens had to know who was handling their case.

Before · for citizens

Fifty doors, no map

Each body had its own forms and its own way of processing requests. Citizens first had to work out where to go.

Before · behind the counter

Email, paper and blind spots

Cases moved between bodies by email and paper, with no visibility of their status. Every new digital service meant a new development project.

The goal

One platform, one model

One place to apply, one way to follow the process and a common model so every body can add its services.

08 · Key decisions

The calls that defined the product.

1

Same request, same states, every channel

One set of states and one history for web, app and counter.

WhyA citizen who starts at a counter and checks the status on their phone should see the same story.

2

Configure services, don’t develop them

Each public body publishes services with its own fields, rules and approvals, inside a shared pattern.

WhyGoing from 150 to 300 services in a year only works if adding one is configuration, not development.

3

Permissions that mirror the real organisation

Seven profiles, from citizen to administrator, each with its journeys, access and segregation of duties.

WhyPublic data and legal accountability: every action with its owner and its audit trail.

4

Evolve the design system, don’t replace it

We extended the government’s design system with new reusable components, accessible from the start.

WhyConsistency for citizens and a foundation the next team can keep building on.

12 · Design and backlog

Designer with Product Owner responsibilities.

Six designers, twelve developers and one shared backlog. I kept the product vision, prioritised the backlog and turned every screen into user stories, acceptance criteria and business rules ready to build.

After launch, I manage change requests: analysis, prioritisation, design and release to production.

Example (simplified)

As an assisted-service operator, I want to submit a request on behalf of a citizen so that people without digital access get the same service.

  • The request is linked to the citizen, not to the operator.
  • The citizen can follow its status in the portal afterwards.
  • The history records which operator submitted it and when.

14 · What I learned

Design the system, not just the screens.

Transformation isn’t delivering screens: it’s leaving an end-to-end, configurable platform the organisation grows on its own. That way, each new service stops being a project and becomes a decision.
